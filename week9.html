Negotiation Skills

This week focused on understanding negotiation skills and how they help people reach agreements that benefit both sides. We learned about the importance of preparation, effective communication and finding solutions that satisfy everyone involved. During the session, we also completed a 30-question quiz and discussed two case studies based on salary negotiation and business negotiation to understand how these skills can be applied in real-life situations.

Key concepts

Negotiation

A structured process in which both sides try to reach a mutually acceptable agreement. Preparation comes first: know your goals, alternatives and information.

BATNA

The Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement. A strong one raises your confidence and bargaining power. Find it by listing your alternatives and judging which is best.

Walk-away threshold

The point beyond which accepting the deal is worse than taking your alternative.

Positions vs interests

A position is what someone says they want, and an interest is why they want it. Focusing on interests opens up more options.

Win-win

Both sides find options that meet their important interests.

Listening and rapport

Restating the other person's point shows you understood, and rapport builds trust.

Salary negotiation

Present evidence of your achievements, responsibilities and market information, and discuss the whole package, not just the figure.

Concessions

Make thoughtful ones and trade them for something valuable.

Contingent contract

An agreement that applies if specified future conditions occur.

Trust and verify

Build trust while checking important facts and commitments.

Deadlock and closing

Explore new options or take a break, and set clear benchmarks and deadlines to bring a negotiation to a close.

Biases and emotion

Both can lead to poor decisions, so avoid reacting without thinking through the consequences.

In class: the case studies

Case study 1: Salary negotiation

Amal, a marketing executive on LKR 120,000 a month, believes the market range is LKR 140,000 to 160,000. Her manager offers LKR 130,000. The case asks about her interests, her BATNA, the evidence she can present and a reasonable counteroffer.

Case study 2: Business negotiation

ABC Solutions wants a one-year software package. The supplier quotes LKR 2.4 million against a budget of LKR 2 million. ABC cares more about technical support, training and flexible payment terms than the lowest price, so the case asks for win-win options and a package deal.

What I Learned

Through this session, I learned that negotiation is not about winning or forcing others to agree, but about finding solutions that benefit both sides. I understood that preparation, active listening and clear communication play an important role in reaching a successful agreement. Learning about BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement) and the walk-away point helped me understand the importance of knowing my options before making decisions. The case studies also showed me that understanding the reasons behind someone's demands can lead to better solutions, such as offering additional benefits or flexible arrangements. I realised that successful negotiation also helps maintain positive relationships between both parties. Since I have limited experience in negotiation, I want to practise these skills and become more confident when handling discussions and making decisions in the future.
